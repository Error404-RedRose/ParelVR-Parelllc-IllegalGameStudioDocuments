THE THREE CLAIMS IN THIS EXCHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. "I did tell him and give him time in advanced, as he even reacted in a thumbs
   up mind you."

   This is the CEO's advance-notice defence. It rests on a thumbs-up reaction.
   Exhibit P-03 shows the reaction, but Discord does not display reaction
   timestamps. The exhibit cannot show when the reaction was added, whether the
   complainant had read the message, or whether the reaction was an
   acknowledgement of the meeting rather than a routine reaction.

   The complainant states the reaction was added after returning from work. The
   reaction is not a meeting schedule, an agenda, a notice period or an attendance
   confirmation. Document 12, section 3.5, sets out the point.

2. "He did not say anything about it until after I issued a warning."

   This is contradicted by the record in Document 12. The complainant posted a
   written leave notice on 2 November at 11:35 PM, approximately eighteen hours
   before the meeting. The complainant also replied on returning from construction
   at 6:55 PM (exhibit clock) and appealed the strike at 7:02 PM. The CEO's own
   announcement stated that physical work was an accepted excuse subject to proof.
   The leave notice was the reason; no direct-message request for further proof
   appears before the strike.

3. "Hes not a freelancer / not to us."

   This is the other side of the contradiction identified in Document 12, section
   3.7. The CEO treats the complainant as outside the contract for protection
   purposes ("not a freelancer", "ur contract doesnt really count") while applying
   the company's mandatory-meeting and strike rules to the complainant. A person
   cannot be outside the contract when it limits the company's obligations and
   inside it when it imposes discipline.
