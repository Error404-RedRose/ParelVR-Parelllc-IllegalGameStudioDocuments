WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The dispute did not begin with the later public statements in Documents 04-06.
It began on 2-3 November 2026, when the CEO of Parelllc / ParelVR imposed a
same-day mandatory meeting and a strike penalty during the complainant's
pre-notified construction leave, then denied that a strike had been given.

This document isolates that sequence. It reproduces the messages as they appear
in the supplied screenshots and in the supplied message log, identifies the
contradictions between the CEO's statements and his own written policy, and
states what the exhibits do and do not prove.

complainant, acted inconsistently: notice was given, the meeting was announced
with only hours of notice, a strike was threatened and communicated, and the
complainant was penalised for being at work after having said they would be at
work.

3. THE CONTRADICTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The following are direct comparisons between the CEO's written statements and
the record. They are the strongest part of this exhibit because they do not
depend on the wider allegations in Documents 01-06 and 09-11.

3.1  "No strikes were given" versus "today ill be striking people"

  Written policy (P-01, 5:23 PM):
    "today ill be striking people for missing meetings"
    "if no proof i will count it as a strike"

  Strike notice received (P-04, 7:02 PM):
    "I have been notified that I have received a strike (1 of 3)"

  Later denial (P-06, 7:57 PM):
    "there was no strikes given so no worries. it was all just warning"
    "it was a warning i issue. nothing bad."

  The CEO's own announcement created a strike regime and stated that missing a
  meeting would be counted as a strike. The complainant was told a strike had
  been issued. The later statement that "there was no strikes given" is
  inconsistent with the written policy and with the notice the complainant
  received. At minimum, the CEO recharacterised the same measure from a strike
  to a warning after the complainant objected.

3.2  "You never reported that to us" versus the written leave notice

  Leave notice (T-01, 2 November, 11:35 PM):
    "I will be unavailable for PVR development for several more days due to
    extensive construction work with my father. We anticipate this will take
    approximately one week."

  Later statement (P-07, 8:00 PM):
    "u never reported that to us. how am i to know you where working yk."

  The notice was posted approximately eighteen hours before the meeting. It was
  in the channel. Even if the CEO did not personally read it, the claim that the
  complainant "never reported" the work is contradicted by the record. The
  CEO's own policy asked for direct-message proof of physical work; the leave
  notice supplied the reason, and no such request appears in the supplied
  record before the strike.

3.3  "I just need proof" versus no opportunity to provide it

  Written procedure (P-01):
    "if you cannot attend due to reasons such as physcial work, medical issue,
    or anything actually reasonable please dm me with PROOF, if no proof i will
    count it as a strike"

  Timeline (exhibit clock):
    5:23 PM  announcement
    5:29 PM  ping
    6:55 PM  complainant returns from construction and replies
    7:02 PM  strike notice

  The complainant was at work for the period in question and replied as soon as
  they returned. The strike was issued within minutes of that reply. On the
  supplied record, no "dm me with PROOF" request appears before the penalty was
  imposed. The complainant's leave notice was already the proof the policy
  asked for.

3.4  "I said ur good" versus "we can take down ur assets"

  Same message (P-06/P-07, 8:03 PM):
    "but since u told me u where working irl, i said ur [good]"
    "well u can leave if thats how ur going to act. we can take donw ur assets
    from the storage."
    "but if your going to act rude/be rude then yes feel free to leave."

  The CEO accepts the work explanation and says the complainant is "good", then
  in the same message threatens to remove the complainant's assets because of
  the complainant's tone. The condition attached to the threat is the
  complainant's objection, not a policy violation. That is the core retaliation
  evidence in this exhibit.

  The record also shows the complainant did not attend at the time of the ping:
  their 6:55 PM reply says they had "just got back from construction, so i can
  attend soon", and the CEO's 8:03 PM message acknowledges "since u told me u
  where working irl, i said ur good". The CEO therefore knew the complainant had
  been at work and had not attended.

3.5  The "advance notice" is a reaction, not a schedule

  CEO's later account (Document 07):
    "i did tell him and give him time in advanced, as he even reacted in a
    thumbs up mind you"

  Exhibit P-03:
    a thumbs-up reaction by KittyFangs.exe on the meeting ping; no reaction
    timestamp; no meeting schedule, agenda or notice period.

  A reaction emoji does not show when it was added, does not show that the
  person read the message, and does not show that the person agreed to attend.
  The complainant states the reaction was added after returning from work. The
  exhibit cannot establish timely notice.

3.6  "It was just a warning" versus a "1 of 3" disciplinary record

  The strike was communicated as "a strike (1 of 3)" (P-04), which describes a
  graduated disciplinary record with two further steps. Calling it "just a
  warning" after the fact does not erase the written threat, the recorded step,
  or the consequence attached to it. The distinction matters because the
  complainant's objection was to being placed on a disciplinary track at all.

3.7  "Your contract does not count" versus "you are subject to our rules"

  CEO statement (P-06, 7:58 PM):
    "for u it doesnt really count ur contract, thats why it was a warning"

  The same exchange treats the complainant as outside the contract for
  protection purposes, while applying the company's mandatory-meeting and
  strike rules to the complainant. A person cannot be outside the contract when
  it limits the company's obligations and inside it when it imposes discipline.
  This is the unilateral-policy problem described in Document 01, pattern 1.

5. LEGAL RELEVANCE - SUMMARY ONLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section is a signpost, not legal advice. The authorities are set out in
Documents 09 (United States) and 10 (international and common law).

The 2-3 November sequence is relevant to:

5.1  Unilateral policy and contract
- A mandatory meeting and strike regime imposed during an existing engagement,
  without a written contract term, may be a unilateral variation, a breach of
  the implied covenant of good faith, or a breach of a separate studio-to-studio
  agreement.
- The CEO's statement that the contract "doesnt really count" for the
  complainant, while applying the policy to the complainant, is evidence of the
  inconsistency.

5.2  Wage deductions and adverse action (if the complainant is an employee)
- A "strike" that withholds pay, points, access or continued engagement may be
  an unlawful deduction or an adverse action. See Document 09, sections 2-3,
  and the state wage-deduction authorities (for example Cal. Lab. Code §§ 221,
  224; N.Y. Lab. Law § 193).
- For freelancers, the New York Freelance Isn't Free Act and the California
  Freelance Worker Protection Act provide written-contract, timely-payment and
  anti-retaliation protections regardless of employee status. See Document 09,
  section 1.

5.3  Retaliation
- The asset-removal threat followed the complainant's objection to the policy.
  If the objection is protected activity, the timing and the conditional threat
  support a retaliation theory. See Document 09, section 3; Document 10,
  section 9.
- "Protected activity" is the contested element. Raising a contract or policy
  concern is the complainant's theory; the CEO will likely argue it was
  insubordination.

5.4  Tortious interference and conversion
- A threat to remove a contractor's stored assets can support a claim for
  conversion, trespass to chattels, breach of contract or tortious interference
  with business relations if the assets were removed or withheld. See Document
  09, section 9.
- Preserve the asset list, storage records and any deletion notices.

5.5  Misrepresentation
- The statement "there was no strikes given" is contradicted by the written
  announcement and the strike notice. If the statement was relied on to the
  complainant's detriment, it may support a misrepresentation theory. See
  Document 09, section 6.

5.6  Working time, rest and health
- Same-day mandatory meetings that disrupt sleep and rest may engage working
  time and health-and-safety rules in the UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand,
  Ireland and the EU. See Document 10, sections 1-6. The application depends on
  the complainant's location, the company's location and the contract.

6. AUTHENTICATION AND PRESERVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The images are secondary captures of a Discord interface. To make them usable,
preserve the originals and the context:

1. Keep the original PNG files unedited. Do not crop, annotate or re-save over
   them. Work on copies.
2. Record the capture device, the Discord account, the server and channel, and
   the timezone of the device.

   the channel name, date dividers and surrounding messages are visible.
5. Preserve the reaction state and, if possible, the reaction users list.
6. Record SHA-256 hashes at capture and re-verify them before any filing. The
   current hashes are in IMAGE-MANIFEST.txt.
7. Keep a chain-of-custody note: who captured each image, when, on what device,
   and where the original is stored.
8. Do not upload private data, addresses, phone numbers, account numbers or
   identity documents.
