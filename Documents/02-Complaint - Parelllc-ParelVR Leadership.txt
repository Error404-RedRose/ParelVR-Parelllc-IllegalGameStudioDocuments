CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This document is one case study within a wider public notice. It is the
complainant's own account of being penalised and banned after objecting to a new
staff policy.

It is NOT a claim by Twilight Studio of ownership over Parelllc, ParelVR or their
products. It is NOT a lawsuit or a court order. Twilight Studio is one affected
contractor among many. The same treatment is reported by other organisations,
studios, developers, freelancers, investors and users, whose documents are being
gathered for Document 11 and future filings.

The complainant states that DM records, publicly saved documents and sealed
documents held by other members document comparable conduct. Those records are
referred to in Document 01 and are not reproduced here.

The names "eyeballs", "KittyFangs.exe" and the Twilight Studio Head Administrator
refer to the same individual. The original complaint is preserved below. A dated
evidentiary preface and an annex have been added to connect it to the screenshots
in Document 12. The original wording has not been altered.

Note: This passage was supplied twice in the source material. The two copies were
identical apart from incidental formatting (for example "$7 million" versus
"a$7million"). They have been consolidated here into one authoritative copy.
No fact, allegation or instruction has been removed.

POST-SUBMISSION EVIDENTIARY PREFACE (2026-11-06)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When this complaint was first written, the 2-3 November 2026 screenshots had not
been indexed. They are now in Document 12 and in the Image FIles folder. They
establish the following sequence, which is the factual basis for the complaint:

1. On 2 November 2026 at 11:35 PM the complainant posted a written leave notice:
   unavailable for several days because of construction work with their father;
   approximately one week. The notice was in the channel approximately eighteen
   hours before the meeting.

2. On 3 November 2026 the Crispy account announced: "we are starting the join ups
   for meetings, i expect everyone in Parel LLC to joion this meeting, today ill
   be striking people for missing meetings... this is a mandatory meeting. if you
   cannot attend due to reasons such as physcial work, medical issue, or anything
   actually reasonable please dm me with PROOF, if no proof i will count it as a
   strike, thank you, @everyone".

3. Gremix pinged the complainant the same day: "we are starting a meeting here
   shortly". The complainant replied on returning from construction: "i just got
   back from construction, so i can attend soon".

4. The complainant was notified of "a strike (1 of 3) for missing a scheduled
   meeting" and appealed in writing, giving the work hours (9:00 AM to 5:00 PM)
   and the prior notice.

5. The CEO replied: "there was no strikes given so no worries. it was all just
   warning". He also said: "for u it doesnt really count ur contract, thats why it
   was a warning"; "u never reported that to us. how am i to know you where working
   yk"; and "well u can leave if thats how ur going to act. we can take donw ur
   assets from the storage".

6. The complainant objected that the meeting was unplanned, that a one-week notice
   is required for strikes, that they were on scheduled leave, and that the
   company was treating a development crew like an EMS or military unit.

7. The complainant gave formal notice that a written contract would be issued
   before Friday and that the CEO's actions constituted a complete termination of
   the existing arrangement if not resolved.

The evidentiary significance is not that the parties disagreed. It is that the
CEO's later account is inconsistent with his own written policy and with the
complainant's written notice. Document 12 sets out seven specific contradictions,
including the denial of a strike that the CEO's own announcement had threatened,
and the statement that the complainant never reported the work despite the
11:35 PM leave notice.

Note on figures: the source statements use "$7 million" (this document),
"$1million-plus home" (the leave notice) and "72 million dollars worth of rental
properties" (the 8:59 PM message). These appear to describe different assets (a
construction project, a home renovation and a rental portfolio) rather than one
figure. The complaint does not rely on a single valuation. The material fact is
the construction work and the leave notice, not the valuation.

ANNEX B - WHAT THIS DOCUMENT ADDS TO THE RECORD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The original complaint made four factual claims that the 2-3 November exhibits
now support directly:

1. The complainant had taken leave for construction work before the meeting. The
   leave notice is dated 2 November at 11:35 PM (Exhibit T-01).
2. The meeting was unplanned and announced with same-day notice. The announcement
   is dated 3 November (Exhibit P-01).
3. A strike was threatened and communicated. The announcement threatens strikes
   (P-01), and the complainant was told of "a strike (1 of 3)" (P-04).
4. The complainant objected and was met with a denial and a threat to remove
   assets (P-06 and P-07).

The original complaint's characterisation of the policy as "Among Us" style and
as an "EMS hotline" is the complainant's opinion. The exhibits supply the
underlying facts: same-day notice, a written strike threat, a pre-existing leave
notice and a conditional asset-removal threat.
