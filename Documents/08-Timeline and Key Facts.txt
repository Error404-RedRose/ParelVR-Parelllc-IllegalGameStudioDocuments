SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This chronology concerns a pattern of conduct alleged against the CEO of
Parelllc / ParelVR ("Crispy" / "CrispyAl_T" / "Gremix") reported to have affected
multiple organisations, studios, contractors, investors, users and banned
members. It is not limited to the complainant, and it is not a Twilight Studio
claim of ownership over Parelllc or ParelVR.

The precipitating event was not the later public dispute. It was the CEO's
decision on 3 November 2026 to impose a same-day mandatory meeting and a strike
penalty during the complainant's pre-notified construction leave, and then to
deny that a strike had been given. Document 12 sets out the screenshots,
timestamps and contradictions.

3. CORE FACTUAL ASSERTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Policy imposed outside contract
- A staff attendance rule requiring unannounced "emergency meetings" is alleged
  to have been imposed without a formal contract, without notice and without the
  consent of partner studios.
- Members who missed the meetings, or did not "say hi", allegedly received a 1/3
  strike.
- The rule was pressed during the complainant's staff leave, which the other side
  allegedly had not read.
- The obligation is described as absolute despite there being no established
  contract.

3.1A The 3 November meeting and strike (primary evidence)
- On 3 November 2026 the Crispy account posted: "we are starting the join ups for
  meetings, i expect everyone in Parel LLC to joion this meeting, today ill be
  striking people for missing meetings... this is a mandatory meeting. if you
  cannot attend due to reasons such as physcial work, medical issue, or anything
  actually reasonable please dm me with PROOF, if no proof i will count it as a
  strike, thank you, @everyone" (Exhibit P-01).
- The meeting was announced on the same day it was held. No schedule, agenda or
  notice period appears in the record.
- Gremix pinged the complainant: "Hey @KittyFangs.exe we are starting a meeting
  here shortly" (Exhibit P-02).
- The complainant had posted a written leave notice the previous night at 11:35
  PM, stating they would be unavailable for several days because of construction
  work (Exhibit T-01).
- The complainant replied at 6:55 PM: "i just got back from construction, so i
  can attend soon" (Exhibit P-02).
- The complainant was then notified of "a strike (1 of 3) for missing a scheduled
  meeting" and appealed it at 7:02 PM, giving the work hours (9:00 AM to 5:00 PM)
  and the prior notice (Exhibit P-04).
- The CEO replied at 7:57 PM: "there was no strikes given so no worries. it was
  all just warning" (Exhibit P-06).
- The CEO also said: "for u it doesnt really count ur contract, thats why it was
  a warning" (Exhibit P-06).
- The CEO said: "u never reported that to us. how am i to know you where working
  yk" (Exhibit P-07), although the leave notice was in the record.
- The CEO then said: "well u can leave if thats how ur going to act. we can take
  donw ur assets from the storage" (Exhibit P-06).
- The complainant states the thumbs-up reaction the CEO later cites as "advance
  notice" was added after returning from work. Discord does not display reaction
  timestamps; the exhibit cannot establish when it was added (Exhibit P-03).

3.1B The contradictions in the CEO's account
The primary exhibit identifies seven direct inconsistencies between the CEO's
statements and the record (Document 12, section 3):

1. "There was no strikes given" versus "today ill be striking people" and "i
   will count it as a strike" in the CEO's own announcement.
2. "You never reported that to us" versus the 11:35 PM leave notice posted the
   night before.
3. "I just need proof" versus the strike being issued without a direct-message
   proof request and after the complainant had already given the reason.
4. "I said ur good" versus "we can take donw ur assets from the storage" in the
   same message.
5. "Advance notice" via a thumbs-up reaction versus no reaction timestamp and no
   meeting schedule.
6. "It was just a warning" versus a communicated "strike (1 of 3)" disciplinary
   step.
7. "Your contract does not really count" versus applying the company's mandatory
   meeting and strike rules to the complainant.

3.2 Bans and retaliation
- Members are alleged to have been banned without discussion, a stated breach or an
  appeal.
- The complainant was allegedly banned after offering professional advice and
  disputing the policy; the complainant states no rules were broken during their
  time with the organisation.
- The complainant's main account was allegedly also banned from the Discord server.
- The complainant's hub world is stated to remain live on ParelVR.
- The complainant removed Parelllc from their server as a temporary punishment.
- The 3 November exchange adds a direct retaliation indicator: the CEO accepted
  the work explanation ("i said ur good") and then threatened to remove the
  complainant's assets because of the complainant's tone.

3.3 Fraud, credit and content
- Contributors are alleged to be credited and then removed from the project.
- The owner allegedly claims sole ownership of the software.
- ChilloutVR supplied a framework for private use only; it is allegedly presented
  publicly as the owner's own work.
- A game generated with AI (described as a Claude rewrite) is allegedly claimed as
  original.
- Multiple worlds are alleged to have been ported illegally to the owner's server.
- Proprietary code is alleged to have been appropriated from developers who
  prohibited public distribution.
- Content and records are alleged to have been illegally edited.
- Contributors allegedly worked without agreements or contracts.
- The Steam listing in Exhibit T-03 names "Parel LLC, Twilight Studio" as
  developer. It is offered as evidence of public credit and contribution, not as
  proof of ownership or of the scope of the contribution.

3.4 Funding, governance and cooperation
- The registered owner is described as 18 years old with no legal background.
- The company is described as a freelancer platform falsely portrayed as a legally
  established, authorised game studio.
- Funding is alleged to come from a friend expecting a return on investment.
- Staff are alleged to be removed impulsively, without reference to policies or
  legal agreements, and to suppress dissent.
- The CEO is alleged to have failed to cooperate with affected organisations and
  members, and to have failed to return or account for invested time and money.

3.5 Security
- The server is stated to run Linux and to use hacking tools that provide the
  owner full access to and control over connected PCs.

3.6 Legal exposure asserted
- Alleged unlawful activity involving five other VR game providers, including
  VRChat and additional titles under litigation with Parelllc.
- Alleged breaches of contract and other illegal conduct.
- Alleged illicit VRChat modifications that compromise user accounts, with Discord
  archives cited as corroboration.
- Asserted jurisdiction: international and domestic law governing freelancing,
  online digital content creation and employment.
- Stated intent to consolidate the five implicated parties into one case.

The statutory authorities that may apply are set out in Documents 09 (United
States) and 10 (international and common law). No court order exists.

4A. AUTHENTICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The image files are secondary captures of a Discord interface. The originals are
preserved unedited. SHA-256 hashes and capture notes are in
Image FIles/IMAGE-MANIFEST.txt. The transcript in Document 12 was produced with
the Windows OCR engine and checked against the supplied message log; OCR can
misread names and digits, and the images govern.

Before filing, preserve:
- the original PNG files and their hashes;
- a Discord JSON export of the channel, if available;
- the server, channel, account and device timezone;
- the full scroll around each message;
- the reaction state and, where possible, the reaction users list;
- a chain-of-custody note (who captured what, when, on what device, stored where).
