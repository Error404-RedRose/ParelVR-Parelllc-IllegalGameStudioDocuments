DOCUMENT 10 - LEGAL FRAMEWORK: INTERNATIONAL AND COMMON LAW
POTENTIAL AUTHORITIES OUTSIDE THE UNITED STATES

READ THIS FIRST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is a research index, not legal advice and not a court order. It lists statutes,
regulations and instruments that counsel may evaluate if the facts alleged in
Documents 01-08 are proven. Listing an instrument does not mean it applies, that a
claim will succeed, or that any court or regulator has ruled. Applicability depends
on where the affected person works, where the service is offered, the contracts in
place and the choice-of-law clauses. Verify the current text with a lawyer qualified
in the relevant jurisdiction.

Common-law jurisdictions covered: United Kingdom, Canada (common-law provinces),
Australia, New Zealand and Ireland. The European Union is included because it sets
mandatory rules for services offered to EU users, and because Ireland is both
common-law and EU. Quebec and Scotland are mixed/civil-law systems and are noted
where relevant.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. UNITED KINGDOM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Employment and worker status
- Employment Rights Act 1996 (ERA 1996):
  - Unfair dismissal, s.94; qualifying period generally 2 years, s.108.
  - Automatically unfair dismissal for whistleblowing, s.103A.
  - Detriment for whistleblowing, s.47B; remedy s.48.
  - Written particulars, s.1.
- Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 (inserted the whistleblowing provisions into
  ERA 1996).
- Working Time Regulations 1998 (SI 1998/1833).
- National Minimum Wage Act 1998.
- Employment Relations Act 1999.
- Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992.
- Status: "worker" is broader than "employee" (Uber BV v Aslam [2021] UKSC 5).
  Many gig and freelance arrangements can still attract worker protections.

Equality and harassment
- Equality Act 2010 (protected characteristics; harassment ss.26-27; victimisation
  s.27).
- Protection from Harassment Act 1997 (civil and criminal; course of conduct).

Commercial and corporate
- Companies Act 2006: directors' duties ss.171-177, including s.172 (promote the
  success of the company) and s.174 (reasonable care, skill and diligence).
- Insolvency Act 1986: s.212 (misfeasance), s.214 (wrongful trading), s.238-239
  (transactions at an undervalue/preferences).
- Bribery Act 2010.
- Modern Slavery Act 2015 (supply-chain statements).
- Fraud Act 2006: s.1 fraud; s.2 false representation; s.3 failing to disclose;
  s.4 abuse of position.
- Computer Misuse Act 1990: ss.1-3 (unauthorised access, unauthorised acts,
  unauthorised access with intent).
- Consumer Rights Act 2015 (digital content; goods; services).
- Online Safety Act 2023 (illegal and harmful content; age assurance).

Intellectual property and data
- Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 (CDPA 1988).
- Trade Marks Act 1994.
- Defamation Act 2013 (serious harm threshold, s.1; truth s.2; honest opinion s.3;
  public interest s.4; website operators s.5).
- Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR.
- Limitation Act 1980: contract and tort generally 6 years; defamation 1 year.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  dismissal for federally regulated employers).
- Canadian Human Rights Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. H-6.
- Competition Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-34 (false advertising, deceptive marketing).
- Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA), S.C. 2000,
  c. 5.
- Copyright Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-42.

  criminal harassment; s.342.1 unauthorised use of computer.

Ontario (example province)

  pay, reprisal).
- Occupational Health and Safety Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. O.1 (reprisal).
- Human Rights Code, R.S.O. 1990, c. H.19.

Quebec (civil law)
- Civil Code of Québec (obligations, contract, extracontractual liability).
- Act respecting labour standards, CQLR c. N-1.1.
- Charter of Human Rights and Freedoms, CQLR c. C-12.

3. AUSTRALIA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth):
  - General protections, Part 3-1 (ss.340-352), including adverse action for
    exercising a workplace right.
  - Sham contracting, s.357.
  - Discrimination, s.351.
  - Unfair dismissal, s.772; qualifying periods apply.
- Corporations Act 2001 (Cth): directors' duties ss.180-184 (care and diligence,
  good faith, improper use of position, improper use of information).
- Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth), Schedule 2 (Australian Consumer Law):
  s.18 misleading or deceptive conduct; s.29 false representations; unfair contract
  terms.
- Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), including the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme.
- Copyright Act 1968 (Cth).
- Online Safety Act 2021 (Cth).
- Criminal Code Act 1995 (Cth): fraudulent conduct provisions (Div 134) and
  computer offences (Part 10.7).
- State and territory work health and safety, discrimination and defamation laws.

6. EUROPEAN UNION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Data and platform rules
- General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR): lawful basis, Art. 6;
  transparency, Arts. 12-14; access, Art. 15; erasure, Art. 17; security, Art. 32;
  remedies, Art. 79-82.
- Digital Services Act, Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (notice-and-action, transparency,
  risk assessments, researcher access).
- Platform Work Directive (EU) 2024/2831 (algorithmic management, employment
  status presumption; transposition by 2 December 2026).

Employment and contract
- Directive (EU) 2019/1152 on transparent and predictable working conditions.
- Written Statement Directive (replaced by 2019/1152).
- Posting of Workers Directive 96/71/EC and Enforcement Directive 2014/67/EU.
- Rome I Regulation (EC) 593/2008 (choice of law in contracts).
- Rome II Regulation (EC) 864/2007 (non-contractual obligations).
- Brussels I bis Regulation (EU) 1215/2012 (jurisdiction and enforcement).

Consumer and commercial
- Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC.
- Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU.
- Digital Content and Digital Services Directive (EU) 2019/770.
- Unfair Contract Terms Directive 93/13/EEC.
- Copyright in the Digital Single Market Directive (EU) 2019/790.
- Trade Secrets Directive (EU) 2016/943.
- eCommerce Directive 2000/31/EC (largely replaced by the DSA).

Fundamental rights
- Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union: Art. 7 (private life), Art. 8
  (data protection), Art. 21 (non-discrimination), Art. 31 (fair and just working
  conditions), Art. 47 (effective remedy).
- European Convention on Human Rights (Council of Europe): Art. 6 (fair trial),
  Art. 8 (private life), Art. 10 (expression), Art. 11 (association).
- European Social Charter (revised, 1996).

7. INTERNATIONAL INSTRUMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Human rights
- Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948): Art. 17 (property), Art. 19
  (expression), Art. 20 (association), Art. 23 (work and fair pay), Art. 24 (rest).
- International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (1966): Art. 17 (privacy),
  Art. 19 (expression), Art. 22 (association).
- International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (1966): Art. 6
  (right to work), Art. 7 (fair working conditions), Art. 8 (trade unions).

Labour
- ILO Convention C87 (Freedom of Association and Protection of the Right to
  Organise, 1948).
- ILO C98 (Right to Organise and Collective Bargaining, 1949).
- ILO C100 (Equal Remuneration, 1951).
- ILO C111 (Discrimination, 1958).
- ILO C138 (Minimum Age, 1973).
- ILO C182 (Worst Forms of Child Labour, 1999).
- ILO C158 (Termination of Employment, 1982).
- ILO C175 (Part-Time Work, 1994).
- ILO C177 (Home Work, 1996).

Soft law and guidance
- UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (2011).
- OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises (2023 edition).
- ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work (1998, amended 2022).

Cross-border enforcement
- New York Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards
  (1958).
- Hague Service Convention (1965).
- Hague Choice of Court Convention (2005).
- Brussels I bis (EU) for judgments within the EU.
- There is no general US-UK judgments treaty; enforcement is by comity and state
  law.

8. GAME-SPECIFIC AND CONSUMER ISSUES OUTSIDE THE US
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- UK Consumer Rights Act 2015: digital content must be of satisfactory quality,
  fit for purpose and as described; remedies include repair, replacement, price
  reduction and refund.
- EU Digital Content Directive (EU) 2019/770: conformity, remedies, modification
  rights.
- EU/UK consumer protection: misleading advertising, dark patterns and unfair terms.
- UK Online Safety Act 2023 and EU Digital Services Act 2022/2065: content
  moderation, notice-and-action, transparency and risk assessment.
- Age ratings: PEGI (Europe), ESRB (North America), USK (Germany), CERO (Japan),
  ACB (Australia) - self-regulatory but contractually enforced by platforms.
- Platform distribution agreements (Steam, Epic, consoles) impose content,
  licensing and refund obligations that are contractual.

10. HUMAN-RIGHTS AND BUSINESS-CONDUCT FRAMING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Where a statute does not fit, the archive may still rely on human-rights and
business-conduct standards in advocacy, regulatory complaints and ESG reporting:

- Right to fair working conditions: UDHR Art. 23; ICESCR Art. 7; EU Charter
  Art. 31.
- Freedom of expression and association: UDHR Arts. 19-20; ICCPR Arts. 19, 22;
  ECHR Arts. 10-11.
- Privacy and data protection: UDHR Art. 12; ICCPR Art. 17; GDPR; ECHR Art. 8.
- Property and credit: UDHR Art. 17; national copyright and contract law.
- Business and human rights: UN Guiding Principles (2011); OECD Guidelines (2023).
- Labour rights: ILO core conventions.

These instruments are persuasive and, in some countries, directly enforceable
against public bodies. Against a private company they are strongest when combined
with a domestic statute, a regulator complaint or a platform policy report.

11. LIMITS OF THIS DOCUMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- No court or regulator has issued any order in this matter as of the archive date.
- Applicability depends on the location of the affected person, the location of the
  service, the contract and any choice-of-law or arbitration clause.
- Common-law and civil-law systems differ; do not assume a US theory transfers.
- Enforcement of a judgment across borders is a separate and often difficult step.
- Nothing here is a substitute for advice from a lawyer qualified in the relevant
  jurisdiction.
