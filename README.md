## Public notice, not a lawsuit

This repository exists to give the CEO of Parelllc / ParelVR a documented opportunity to
respond and resolve the issues **before the game is published on Steam**.

It is:

- a public record of allegations raised by **many organisations and individuals**, not one
  studio;
- an index of documents being gathered for potential court use;
- an open reporting point where affected parties can add their accounts through **Issues**;
- a request for cooperation, credit, payment and the withdrawal of policies imposed
  outside contract.

It is **not**:

- a claim that Twilight Studio owns Parelllc, ParelVR or their products;
- a statement that any allegation has been proven;
- a private dispute between Twilight Studio and Parelllc;

## The precipitating event: 3 November 2026

The dispute did not begin with the later public statements. It began when the CEO imposed
a **same-day mandatory meeting and a strike penalty** during the complainant's
**pre-notified construction leave**, then denied that a strike had been given.

The sequence, documented in
[Document 12](Documents/12-Evidence%20Exhibit%20-%20Meeting%20Notice%20and%20Strike%20Chronology.txt):

| Date and time | Event | Exhibit |
|---------------|-------|---------|
| **2 Nov 2026, 11:35 PM** | The complainant posts a written leave notice: unavailable for several days because of construction work with their father; approximately one week. | T-01 |
| **3 Nov 2026, same day** | The Crispy account announces a **mandatory meeting**: *"today ill be striking people for missing meetings... this is a mandatory meeting. if you cannot attend due to reasons such as physcial work, medical issue, or anything actually reasonable please dm me with PROOF, if no proof i will count it as a strike, thank you, @everyone"* | P-01 |
| **3 Nov 2026, same day** | Gremix pings: *"we are starting a meeting here shortly"*. | P-02 |
| **3 Nov 2026, same day** | The complainant replies on returning from construction: *"i just got back from construction, so i can attend soon"*. | P-02 |
| **3 Nov 2026, same day** | The complainant is notified of *"a strike (1 of 3) for missing a scheduled meeting"* and appeals it in writing, giving the work hours (9:00 AM–5:00 PM) and the prior notice. | P-04 |
| **3 Nov 2026, same day** | The CEO says: *"there was no strikes given so no worries. it was all just warning"*; *"for u it doesnt really count ur contract"*; *"u never reported that to us"*; and *"we can take donw ur assets from the storage."* | P-06, P-07 |

### What the exhibits show

1. **Same-day notice.** The meeting was announced on the day it was held. No schedule,
   agenda or notice period appears in the record.
2. **Prior written leave.** The complainant had already given written notice of the
   construction work, approximately eighteen hours before the meeting was announced.
3. **Prompt reply.** The complainant replied as soon as they returned from work, and the
   strike was communicated within minutes of that reply.
4. **The strike was threatened and communicated.** The CEO's own announcement explicitly
   threatened strikes, and the complainant was told a strike had been issued.
5. **The denial and the threat.** The CEO said no strike was given, said the complainant's
   contract *"doesnt really count"*, and then threatened to remove the complainant's assets
   in the same exchange in which he said the complainant was *"good"*.

### The documented contradictions

| # | CEO statement | Record |
|---|---------------|--------|
| 1 | *"there was no strikes given"* | The announcement: *"today ill be striking people"*; *"i will count it as a strike"*. The complainant: *"I have been notified that I have received a strike (1 of 3)"*. |
| 2 | *"u never reported that to us"* | The 2 November 11:35 PM leave notice, in the channel approximately eighteen hours before the meeting. |
| 3 | *"i just need proof"* | The strike was issued without a direct-message proof request and after the complainant had already given the reason; the policy's own proof procedure was not followed. |
| 4 | *"i said ur good"* | *"we can take donw ur assets from the storage"* — the threat is conditioned on the complainant's tone, not on a policy violation. |
| 5 | *"i did tell him... he even reacted in a thumbs up"* | The reaction exhibit shows no timestamp; Discord does not display reaction times. A reaction is not a schedule, notice or attendance confirmation. |
| 6 | *"it was all just warning"* | The complainant was told *"a strike (1 of 3)"* — a graduated disciplinary step, not an informal word. |
| 7 | *"for u it doesnt really count ur contract"* | The same exchange applies the company's mandatory-meeting and strike rules to the complainant. He cannot be outside the contract for protection and inside it for discipline. |

**Rules for reading the table**

- The time in a file name is the time displayed in that image. It is not converted.
- The **Parelllc Images** show the CEO-side clock. The **Twilight Studio Images** and the **copied log** show the Eastern Canada clock.
- The observed difference is **one hour**. The complainant is in Eastern Canada; the CEO's side is described as western USA. The geographic labels alone do not fix the offset (western USA can be Pacific or Mountain time); the one-hour figure is what the capture devices show. Record the device timezone for each exhibit; the images govern. The archive does not silently convert a time from one zone to another.
- The leave notice is only on the Twilight clock (T-01 and the copied log). It has no +1:00 comparison.
- The thumbs-up reaction in P-03 has no timestamp in either clock.
- Do not add or subtract the offset without stating which clock you are converting to.
- The sequence is identical on both clocks. The one-hour difference is a display-timezone difference, not a gap in the events.

This matters because the CEO's "advance notice" and "you never reported" claims are tested against the times in each clock, not a mixed calculation.

## Scope: the alleged systemic pattern

| # | Alleged pattern | Description |
|---|-----------------|-------------|
| 1 | **Policy imposed outside contract** | Unannounced "emergency meetings" enforced against freelancers and partner studios, with a **1/3 strike** for missing them or not "saying hi" — without a formal contract, notice or consent. The 3 November 2026 sequence is the primary example: a same-day mandatory meeting, a written strike threat, a pre-existing leave notice that was not applied, and a denial that any strike was given. |
| 2 | **Bans without justification** | Members banned without discussion, a stated breach or an appeal, described as retaliation rather than discipline. |
| 3 | **Fraud and illegally edited content** | Contributed work, credit, records and funds allegedly taken, edited or removed; contributors' names and existence erased while ownership is claimed. |
| 4 | **Failure to cooperate and return** | The CEO allegedly fails to address concerns, cooperate with affected parties, or return and account for invested time, money, work and property. |
| 5 | **Pressure on investors and partners** | Private funding expected to return a profit while partner studios, contractors and users are expelled for raising lawful concerns. |
| 6 | **Security overreach** | A Linux server allegedly running hacking tools that give the owner full access to and control over connected PCs. |
| 7 | **Denial and retaliation after objection** | When the complainant objected in writing and proposed a written contract, the CEO recharacterised the strike as "just a warning", said the contract "doesnt really count", and threatened to remove the complainant's assets from storage. |

The complainant's own experience is presented as **one case study** (Document 02), not the
centre of the archive. Details, sources and the full assertion list are in
[Document 01](Documents/01-Executive%20Summary%20-%20Systemic%20Misconduct.txt) and
[Document 08](Documents/08-Timeline%20and%20Key%20Facts.txt).

## What would need to be proven

| Element | Why it matters | Evidence needed |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Employment or worker status** | Most wage, retaliation and discrimination statutes protect employees or workers, not independent contractors. | Contracts, control indicators, payment structure, integration into the business. |
| **A protected activity** | Retaliation claims need a complaint, report or refusal that the law protects. | The message, email or meeting record where the concern was raised. |
| **Causation and timing** | The ban, strike or asset threat must be linked to the protected activity. | Sequence of events, decision-maker statements, comparator treatment. |
| **The exact policy and notice** | A same-day meeting and strike penalty is judged against the policy in force, the notice given and the consequence attached. | The announcement, the schedule (if any), the written policy, the leave notice and the strike notice. |
| **A contract or promise** | Unpaid-work and unilateral-variation claims need an agreement, promise or unjust enrichment. | SOW, invoice, DM agreement, payment history, the company's own statements about the contract. |
| **Ownership or licence terms** | IP claims turn on who owned the work and under what licence. | Contracts, EULAs, contribution records, git history, asset licences. |
| **Unauthorised access** | Computer-access claims need proof of access beyond authorisation. | Server logs, configuration, forensic image, access records. |
| **Loss and damages** | Courts need a measurable harm. | Unpaid amounts, lost revenue, removed credit, deleted content, withheld assets. |
| **Authentication** | Screenshots and OCR transcripts must be tied to the originals. | Original files, SHA-256 hashes, Discord JSON export, device and timezone records, chain of custody. |
| **Publication fault** | If the repository is sued for defamation, fault and truth/opinion defences matter. | Sourced documents, right-of-reply records, corrections log. |

The full evidence checklist is at
[Document 09](Documents/09-Legal%20Framework%20-%20United%20States.txt).
